📚 A. Vocabulary

Word Definition Example Sentence Image
regret a feeling of sadness or disappointment about something that happened, or that you wish had been different She still feels regret about missing the final match due to an injury.
nevertheless in spite of what was just said; however The task seemed impossible; nevertheless, the team managed to finish it on time.
assumption something you believe to be true without checking it Making the wrong assumption about what my teammate meant caused a big misunderstanding.
possibility a chance that something might happen or be true There's still a possibility that the team could have won if they hadn't made that mistake.
revival the return of interest in something that had become less popular The revival of board games happened partly because people wanted more face-to-face contact.
misunderstanding a failure to understand something correctly, causing confusion Their misunderstanding about the deadline caused the whole project to be delayed.
clarify to make something clearer or easier to understand Could you clarify what you meant by "send it soon"?
excerpt a short section taken from a longer text, speech, or recording We listened to an excerpt from the radio show to practice understanding new words.
vary to be different or to change from one situation to another Athletes' earnings can vary a great deal depending on the sport and the country.
restate to say something again, usually in clearer or different words In her conclusion, she restated her opinion using different words.
stood a chance had a real possibility of succeeding or winning Nobody thought the underdog team stood a chance against the world champions.
tournament a series of contests between many players or teams Thousands of fans watched the e-sports tournament live in the stadium.
sponsorship financial support given to an event, team, or person, usually for advertising The team could travel to the championship thanks to a sponsorship from a tech company.
gifted having a natural, often exceptional, ability or talent Even highly gifted people in other professions often earn far less than top athletes.
delighted feeling great pleasure or happiness I was delighted to finally understand the joke once someone explained the new word.
hidden kept out of sight or not easily noticed The players had to find the hidden treasures before the island sank.
profits the financial gain made from a business activity Top athletes' salaries are often justified by the profits they generate through advertising.
contract a written legal agreement between two or more parties The player signed a sponsorship contract worth millions of dollars.
broadcast to send out a program on TV, radio, or online The championship game was broadcast live in over fifty countries.
contradict to say or show that something is not true, or to disagree with someone The new evidence seemed to contradict everything the coach had said earlier.

🧠 B. Grammar: Could have, should have, and would have

What it is / what it's for: These are past modal verbs used to talk about hypothetical past situations — things that were possible but didn't happen, things you now wish had been different, or imagined results of a different past action.

When it is used: * Could have + past participle → to talk about past possibility or ability that wasn't used. * Should have / shouldn't have + past participle → to talk about regrets, things that were the right or wrong thing to do, but didn't happen that way. * Would have + past participle → to talk about an imagined result in the past, usually connected to an unreal past condition.

Structure / Rule

Subject + modal (could / should / would) + have + past participle

  • Affirmative form: Subject + modal + have + past participle
    • She could have won the match, but she got injured.
  • Negative form: Subject + modal + not (couldn't / shouldn't / wouldn't) + have + past participle
    • He shouldn't have missed that easy shot.
  • Interrogative form: Modal + subject + have + past participle?
    • Could she have scored if she had jumped higher?

Three original sentences:

  1. I could have finished the project earlier if I had started studying sooner.
  2. You shouldn't have told her the surprise before the party.
  3. If the team had trained harder, they would have won the championship.

In this unit, I learned a lot of new vocabulary related to sports, competition, and games, as well as how to use past modal verbs like could have, should have, and would have to talk about regrets and past possibilities. I also developed my listening skills by using context to understand new words, and I practiced communicating clearly in a group to avoid misunderstandings.

One activity I found difficult was writing the opinion essay, because I had trouble structuring my paragraphs clearly and connecting opposing points of view using linking words. I also need to improve my pronunciation of stressed syllables, especially in words like compete and competitive. To improve these difficulties, I plan to practice writing short paragraphs every day and record myself speaking to check my pronunciation and fluency.
